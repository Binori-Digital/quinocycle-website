## How Carton Box Sizes Are Written (L × W × H)

Every carton size is quoted as **Length × Width × Height**, in millimetres here in Malaysia:

- **Length (L)** — the longer side of the box opening.
- **Width (W)** — the shorter side of the opening.
- **Height (H)** — from the opening down to the base.

So a box listed as 440 × 310 × 310 mm opens on a 440 × 310 mm face and is 310 mm deep. Two things trip people up constantly:

1. **Internal vs external dimensions.** Suppliers quote internal (usable) dimensions — what your goods must fit inside. External dimensions are 5–10 mm bigger per side for single wall and more for double wall. **Couriers measure external** — that's what your shipping fee is based on.
2. **The same box, different height.** Many models share a footprint but differ in height. Our Q440 and A3 are both 440 × 310 mm at the opening, but the Q440 is 310 mm tall and the A3 is 230 mm. Always check the third number.

## Matching Size to Contents: The 10–20mm Rule

The right box leaves **10–20 mm of space per side** — enough for a layer of paper or bubble wrap, not enough for contents to build momentum and slam around in transit.

- **XS (170 mm class):** cosmetics, accessories, phone cases, jewellery. Anything that would drown in a normal parcel box.
- **S (310–390 mm class):** the e-commerce sweet spot. Books, clothing, shoes, stationery. The JPM's long shallow shape (380 × 185 × 250 mm) is cut for A4 files and documents standing upright.
- **M (440–540 mm class):** household goods, kitchen items, small appliances. This is also where heavy contents live — a medium box of books or plates is at the top end of safe lifting weight already. For fragile loads, pick the double wall A3; the [single vs double wall guide](/blog/single-wall-vs-double-wall-vs-triple-wall-carton/) explains exactly when the extra RM1.50 is worth it.
- **L (590 mm class):** the moving-house workhorse. Big enough to swallow a wardrobe shelf, still liftable when packed sensibly.
- **XL (485 × 485 × 500 mm):** bulky-but-light only — pillows, comforters, soft toys. Fill an XL with books and it will fail (and so will your back).
- **XXL (760–800 mm class):** cargo and freight. The BW800's tall narrow shape suits standing goods; the T760 cube maximises volume for export and equipment.

> **Rule of thumb:** heavy goes small, light goes big. The heavier the contents per litre, the smaller the box should be. That single sentence prevents most packing disasters we hear about.

## Common Size Mistakes (We See These Weekly)

- **Measuring the item, not the item + padding.** Your vase is 280 mm tall, so you order a 290 mm box — then there's no room for wrapping. Add padding allowance *before* choosing.
- **Ignoring height.** Buyers match the footprint and forget the third dimension. A 310 mm-tall box won't close over a 330 mm rice cooker.
- **Assuming external = internal.** Your 310 mm item does not fit a box with 310 mm *external* width. Quoted catalog sizes are internal; when in doubt, ask.
- **One size for everything.** Sixty large boxes for a house move sounds efficient until half of them are dangerously heavy and the other half half-empty. A sensible move mixes S, M and L — our [moving box guide](/kotak-pindah-rumah/) and [English carton hub](/carton-box/) both show recommended mixes.
- **Ordering blind for volumetric weight.** Couriers charge on (L × W × H in cm) ÷ 6000, using external dimensions. One size class down can drop your parcel a whole price tier. Our [dimensional weight explainer](/blog/ilmu-pembungkusan-berat-dimensi-0423/) walks through the maths.

## How to Choose in 60 Seconds

1. Measure your item: L × W × H in mm.
2. Add 20–40 mm total per dimension for padding (more for fragile).
3. Find the smallest catalog box that clears those numbers on all three dimensions.
4. Heavy contents (books, tools, canned goods)? Cap at size M and prefer double wall.
5. Buying for a move? Estimate quantity first — 50–70 boxes for a 3-bedroom home — then split roughly 30% S, 40% M/L, 30% specialty.
