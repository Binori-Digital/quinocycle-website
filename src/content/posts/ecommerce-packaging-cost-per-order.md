Ask most online sellers what packaging costs them per order and you'll get a shrug, or a guess that's off by half. The box price they remember; the tape, filler, label pouch and thank-you card they don't. At 500 orders a month, that blind spot quietly eats hundreds of ringgit — often more than their ad budget waste. Let's do the actual maths, then pull the five levers that bring it down.

## The Formula: Everything That Leaves With the Parcel

Your true packaging cost per order is:

**Box + tape + filler + label/pouch + inserts = cost per parcel**

The trick is converting bulk purchases into per-order numbers:

- A **RM5.00 tape roll** that seals ~25 boxes with H-taping = **RM0.20/order** (single-strip sealing stretches it to ~RM0.10).
- A **RM30 bundle of kraft paper** that fills ~150 parcels = **RM0.20/order**.
- **A6 thermal labels** at RM10 per 100 = **RM0.10/order**.
- A **RM15 pack of 100 thank-you cards** = **RM0.15/order**.

None of these look like money individually. Together, they routinely double the "just the box" number sellers carry in their heads.

## The 3–6% Rule: Is Your Cost Healthy?

A useful framing (as an estimate, not gospel): packaging should land around **3–6% of order value**.

- **RM30 average order** → RM0.90–RM1.80 per parcel is healthy.
- **RM50 average order** → RM1.50–RM3.00.
- **RM150 average order** → up to RM4.50–RM9.00 is defensible, especially for fragile or premium goods.

Consistently **above 6–8%**? You're likely paying for oversized boxes, retail markups, or filler compensating for a box that's the wrong size. Well **below 3%**? Check your damage rate before celebrating — a skimped parcel that arrives crushed costs far more than the sen you saved (we'll put numbers on that in an upcoming post on damage-driven returns).

## Five Levers That Cut Cost Per Order

### 1. Right-size the box

Every centimetre of empty space costs you twice: once in filler to occupy it, once in courier charges if the box tips into a higher dimensional-weight bracket. Our [dimensional weight guide](/blog/ilmu-pembungkusan-berat-dimensi-0423/) covers the courier maths — the short version is that couriers charge for box volume, not just grams, so a snug box is a cheaper box on both lines.

### 2. Switch non-fragile SKUs to used cartons

[Used cartons](/used-carton-boxes/) start from RM0.90 versus RM1.10–RM8.00 for new equivalents, and good-grade ones — many double wall — protect clothing, books and homeware just as well. If half your orders can move to used boxes at, say, RM1.30 average saving, that's RM650/month back at 1,000 orders.

### 3. Buy wholesale, not retail

The same box costs RM8–RM12 at a hardware shop and RM1–RM5 direct from a warehouse. Marketplace listings sit somewhere in between once shipping is included. If you're ordering monthly anyway, one consolidated warehouse order (free delivery in KL & Selangor at RM300+, wholesale deals from RM1,000) beats ten small top-ups every time.

### 4. Standardise on 2–3 sizes

Fewer sizes means bigger quantities per size, which unlocks bulk pricing; it also means faster packing and predictable shipping costs. Our [box guide for TikTok Shop and Shopee sellers](/blog/kotak-untuk-bisnes-tiktok-shop-shopee/) walks through picking the right 2–3 sizes for your product mix.

### 5. Use lighter, cheaper filler

Scrunched kraft paper costs less than bubble wrap, weighs less (courier savings again), and fills voids just as well for non-fragile goods. Keep bubble wrap for genuine fragiles only. Towel-wrapped? That's for house moves — for parcels, paper is the workhorse.

> **Do the audit once a quarter:** prices drift, your product mix changes, and the box that was right-sized in January is half-empty by June. A 20-minute quarterly recount of your per-parcel cost keeps all five levers honest.

## What About Poly Mailers and Flyer Bags?

A fair question at this point: if cost is the goal, why use a box at all? Poly mailers (flyer bags) cost RM0.20–RM0.50 each and suit soft, unbreakable goods — a single t-shirt, a scarf, a phone case. If that describes your whole catalog, mailers win on pure cost.

But boxes earn their premium the moment any of these apply: the product can crease, crush or snap; the order bundles multiple items; the unboxing matters for reviews and repeat purchases; or the parcel will be stacked under others in a courier cage (it will). Many sellers run both — mailers for single soft items, a small used carton at RM1.70 for everything else — and let the order contents pick the packaging. What you shouldn't do is force fragile or multi-item orders into bags to chase the last 50 sen; that's how the damage costs in the next section show up.

## Don't Cut What Protects the Product

One warning before you sharpen the pencil too far: packaging cost and damage cost trade off against each other. Dropping from a double-wall to a flimsy single-wall box to save 80 sen is a bad trade if it adds even a 2% damage rate — a single damaged-parcel return typically wipes out the savings from 15–20 parcels. If your product is fragile, spend deliberately on the box (a proper double-wall carton, decent void fill) and save on everything else.
