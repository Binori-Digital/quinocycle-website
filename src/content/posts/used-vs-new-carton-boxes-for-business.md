If your business ships, stores or moves stock, cartons are a line item you pay for every single month — and one of the few where a 50% saving is genuinely available without changing anything your customer notices. The catch: knowing which shipments can go in used boxes and which genuinely need new. This guide gives you the numbers, the decision rules and a simple fleet strategy used by SMEs across the Klang Valley.

## The Cost Gap: What Businesses Actually Pay Per Box

|---|---|---|---|
| Small | MG — 380 × 230 × 270 mm, RM1.70 | A4 — 310 × 220 × 220 mm, RM3.00 | ~43% |

Note what the table does not show: a quality gap. The used boxes above are all double wall — twin-layer fluted board originally manufactured for factory and import loads. Graded properly, they carry weight comparably to new single wall retail boxes. If you want the engineering behind that claim, our [ECT and Mullen test guide](/blog/packaging-knowledge-ect-mullen-burst-test-0429/) explains how box strength is actually measured.

Scale it up and the gap compounds. A business moving 500 small parcels a month spends roughly **RM850 on used MG cartons versus RM1,500** on equivalent new stock — call it RM7,800 a year back in the business, from one SKU.

## Where Used Cartons Are the Obvious Choice

Three categories of movement where paying for new boxes adds no value:

### Internal transfers

Stock moving between your shop lots, from supplier to your store room, or between office floors. Nobody unboxes these but your own staff. This is the clearest used-carton use case there is — the box is pure logistics, zero marketing.

### Storage

Archived documents, off-season stock, spare parts on racking. A box sitting on a shelf needs stacking strength and dry board, not a pristine surface. Good-grade [used carton boxes](/used-carton-boxes/) handle this at a fraction of the cost — just avoid boxes with water stains or soft walls, which we cover in our used-carton grading guide.

### Non-branded shipping

Marketplace orders on Shopee, Lazada or TikTok Shop where the buyer's expectation is set by the product, not the parcel. A clean, sturdy used carton with proper tape and labelling ships thousands of Malaysian parcels daily without a single complaint. If packaging is not a stated part of your brand promise, this lane is open.

> **Procurement tip:** Grade matters more than "used vs new". A once-used double wall factory carton beats a flimsy new single wall box for heavy goods. Inspect for hard walls, intact corners and no water marks — or buy from a supplier who grades before selling.

## When New Boxes Are Non-Negotiable

We sell used cartons for a living, so take this at face value: some lanes should never run on them.

- **Customer-facing branded shipments.** If unboxing is part of your customer experience — premium products, gifting, subscription boxes — the carton is marketing material. Use new.
- **Export.** Uniform new cartons stack predictably in containers, present cleanly at inspection, and avoid any question marks at the receiving end. From RM1.10 for small new boxes up to XXL cargo cartons, see the full range on our [carton box hub](/carton-box/).
- **Food and hygiene-sensitive goods.** Anything edible or skin-contact should only travel in new board. No exceptions.
- **Long-term archives (1 year+).** New board starts its storage life at full strength and lasts longer before degrading.

## The Sustainability Angle Your Customers Increasingly Notice

There is a second reason Malaysian SMEs are shifting to used cartons, beyond cost: reuse is genuinely the greener option. Recycling cardboard into new board requires re-pulping, de-inking and re-manufacturing — an energy-intensive industrial loop. Reusing an intact carton skips all of it; the box simply does another job before it is eventually pulped.

For businesses starting to answer ESG questions from corporate clients or marketplace programmes, "we ship internal and standard orders in reused cartons" is a concrete, honest claim — no certifications to buy, no greenwashing risk, and it happens to save money rather than cost it. Frame it plainly in your sustainability note and let the practice speak.

## The 80/20 Mixed-Fleet Strategy

The businesses that get this right rarely go 100% used or 100% new. They run a deliberate mix:

| Fleet share | Box type | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| ~80% | Used cartons (RM0.90–RM6.00) | Transfers, storage, marketplace and non-branded shipping |
| ~20% | New cartons (RM1.10–RM9.00) | Branded orders, export, food, fragile premium goods |

Three steps to implement it this month:

1. **Audit one month of box usage.** Tag every carton movement as customer-facing or not. Most SMEs find 70–90% of volume is internal or non-branded.
2. **Standardise on 2–3 used models** for the bulk lane — consistent dimensions make stacking, quoting couriers and reordering easier. Popular business picks: MG (small, RM1.70) for parcels, Yellow (medium, RM3.50) for general goods, PDC (large, RM5.00) for bulk transfers.
3. **Keep a small new-box buffer** for the premium lane so nobody "borrows" scruffy stock for a branded shipment at 5pm on a deadline.

Review quarterly. If complaint rates on used-carton lanes stay at zero — they almost always do — you can push the ratio further. Businesses new to used stock sometimes start conservative at 50/50, watch a quarter of damage and complaint data, then shift; the numbers usually do the persuading. And if you are unsure which lane a given shipment belongs to, one question settles it: would the customer notice or care what the box looks like? If not, it rides used.
