A dropped carton of mugs costs RM50. A dropped server can cost a week of downtime and data you cannot buy back. That is why IT equipment deserves its own packing plan inside the wider [office moving checklist](/blog/office-moving-checklist-malaysia/) — one built around backups, numbered kits and boxes that can actually take a knock. Here is the process we see well-run Malaysian offices follow, whether they have an in-house IT person or one overworked "office tech guy".

## Before Anything Is Unplugged: Photograph and Back Up

Two rules come before the first cable is pulled.

**Photograph everything.** The back of every PC, the server rack front and rear, the router and switch ports, the printer connections. Shoot wide, then close-ups of each port cluster. File photos by workstation number (more on numbering below). On reconnection day these photos turn a guessing game into a copying exercise.

**Back up every machine, verify, and back up the backups.** Servers get a full backup plus one off-site or cloud copy. Workstations at minimum have documents synced to cloud storage. The rule is simple: assume any single device could be dropped, and make sure that event would be annoying, not fatal. Do backups 2–3 days before the move, not the night before — verification takes time.

## The Numbered Workstation Kit System

Treat each desk as one kit that travels together and lands together:

1. **Number every workstation** — WS-01, WS-02 — matching your new-office floor plan.
2. **Label every item in the kit** with that number: PC, monitor, keyboard, mouse, dock, desk phone.
3. **Bag the cables per desk.** All of WS-07's cables go into one zip-lock bag labelled WS-07, taped to the monitor carton or packed in a single "cables" box. Never pool cables from multiple desks into one snake pit.
4. **Mark the cartons** on the side: "WS-07 — MONITOR — FRAGILE — THIS WAY UP".

On arrival, movers deliver each kit to the desk bearing its number, and any staff member can reconnect their own station from the photos. Offices that skip numbering spend the first Monday playing mix-and-match with 40 identical black power bricks.

## Choosing Cartons: Double Wall or Original Box, Nothing Less

The original box with its moulded foam is always first choice — if your office kept monitor and PC boxes, use them. For everything else, the rule is double wall only. Single-wall cartons are fine for stationery; they are not fine for a 7 kg monitor stacked three-high in a moving lorry. **Packing technique:** pad the bottom of the carton before the device goes in, keep roughly 5 cm of bubble wrap or foam on every side, and fill voids so nothing shifts when the box is tilted. Monitors travel vertical, never flat — a monitor laid flat takes stacking weight directly on the panel.

> **Tip:** The shake test — lift each packed carton and tilt it gently. If you hear or feel movement inside, open it and add padding. A device that can shift inside its box will find the one pothole between your old office and the new one.

## Anti-Static: When It Matters and When It Doesn't

For closed, assembled equipment — PCs with the case on, monitors, printers — regular bubble wrap outside the casing is fine. Static becomes a real risk with exposed electronics: loose RAM sticks, graphics cards, bare hard drives, motherboards. Those go into anti-static bags (the pink or metallic-silver ones) before any padding, and never directly against regular plastic wrap or styrofoam, both of which build charge. If a machine must be partially disassembled to move, bag each component separately and label the bag with the workstation number.

## Servers and Drives: Last Out, First In, Signed For

Servers are the one category where "let the movers handle it" is the wrong answer.

- **Schedule them last out, first in.** Shut down cleanly following the proper sequence (applications, then databases, then OS), never just pull power.
- **Small racks and tower servers** can travel padded in rigid double-wall or [XXL cargo cartons](/shop/); heavy rack servers should be removed from the rack and packed individually rather than moved rack-and-all.
- **Chain of custody for drives.** Backup drives, NAS units and any bare disks travel in a staff member's car, not the lorry. Keep a simple signed list: drive serial, who packed it, who carried it, who received it at the new office. It sounds bureaucratic until the day one drive of customer data goes missing and nobody knows who touched it last.
- **Reconnect and verify before Monday**: servers up, backups tested, one machine from each department logged in successfully.

## Insurance and Sign-Off, in Plain Terms

Basic mover liability in Malaysia is commonly calculated per kilogram — which values a crushed server about the same as a crushed chair. Before moving day: get your mover's coverage terms in writing, declare high-value equipment specifically, and check whether your office contents policy extends to goods in transit. For expensive server gear, a specific transit insurance rider is often worth the premium. Frame it generally with your broker; policies differ, and the time to learn yours is before the lorry rolls.

Finally, do an asset count at both ends — the numbered kit list doubles as your checklist. Every WS number accounted for, every drive signed in, then release the movers.
